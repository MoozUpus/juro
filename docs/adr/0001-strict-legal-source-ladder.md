# Escalate legal research through a strict source ladder

JURO legal research searches the Indexed Official Corpus first. When indexed Official Coverage is insufficient or indexed semantic selection is unavailable, live research includes both Live Official Search and Secondary Web Research across the public internet. Official discovery runs first and keeps priority in legal analysis; the wider search also runs when live official discovery succeeds. Answers using live or secondary internet sources display a distinct notice above the answer, with source-specific citations. Non-official publications remain contextual evidence rather than validated legislation.

Amended by owner direction on 2026-09-10 to include the wider internet whenever live research is needed. Sufficient indexed answers do not require an additional internet search.

## Consequences

Retrieval tiers must not run speculatively in parallel. An unranked natural-language candidate packet cannot support a Legal Answer: if Coverage Requirement planning or mandatory semantic selection times out, fails, or returns invalid output, JURO discards that packet, records indexed Source Unavailability, and continues to Live Official Search. A literal-query fallback is available only for explicit, unambiguous act-and-article lookup. A valid semantic rejection instead records absent indexed Official Coverage and also continues to Live Official Search; the distinction remains visible in telemetry and user-facing status. If live official evidence is also insufficient or unavailable, JURO returns the corresponding Insufficient-Evidence Result or Source Unavailability rather than conflating the two. The interface should expose concise, honest progress states so the longer sequential path remains understandable without presenting unvalidated content as an answer.
